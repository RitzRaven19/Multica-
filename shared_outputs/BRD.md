## 2. Project Drivers

### 2.1 Business Problem
Customers expect secure digital access to onboarding, accounts, beneficiaries, transfers, statements, notifications, and service requests. Building even a simplified version requires coordinated business analysis, architecture, engineering, testing, evidence management, and defect triage. Student projects often treat these as isolated activities, causing ambiguous requirements, untestable designs, weak handoffs, and disagreement over whether failures are defects or new requests. The project asks four groups to create both an online-banking simulation and specialized agents that support a traceable SDLC, so that an approved business rule can be shown to become a requirement, design element, implementation, test, defect decision, and final evidence record.

### 2.2 Business Objectives
1. Deliver a coherent banking simulation using synthetic identities, accounts, balances, beneficiaries, and payments.
2. Demonstrate customer and bank-operations' journeys with role-based access and auditable state changes.
3. Build four agent families: BRD; technical design/coding/unit testing; system-test/automation; and defect root-cause analysis.
4. Make agent output reviewable, reproducible, schema-valid, source-linked, and subject to human approval.
5. Use security, privacy, accessibility, reliability, and testability as acceptance conditions — not optional polish.
6. Practice governance: baselines, versioning, change control, traceability, evidence, risk management, and retrospectives.

## 3. Constraints, Assumptions and Dependencies
**Constraints**
- No real money movement is permitted; all funds, balances, and transactions are synthetic (source: SA-OOS3).
- No real Aadhaar/PAN verification service may be integrated; identity checks are format validation only (source: SA-OOS2).

**Assumptions**
- Aadhaar and PAN are the only KYC identifier types required for v1 onboarding.
- Account balances, identities, and transactions are entirely synthetic; no integration with real banking rails or identity-verification providers is required.
- "Login" and "Logout" apply uniformly to both Customer and Bank operations roles unless a role-specific journey is later defined.
- Minimum balance, daily transfer cap, and UAT ownership are unresolved; no default values are assumed until they are answered (see OQ-001, OQ-002, OQ-003).
- The data-testid naming convention for automated testing is pending confirmation (OQ-004); these requirements do not depend on a specific convention.
- Beneficiary management referenced in the charter's broader vision is deferred beyond v1, consistent with the scope agreement's exclusion.

**Dependencies**
- This BRD is Stage 1 of the Group 2 SDLC pipeline; there is no upstream `approved/` dependency for this run — charter.md, scope_agreement.md, and BRD_template.txt are the only inputs.
- Downstream design, coding, testing, and defect-analysis agents depend on this BRD, business_rules.json, and open_questions.json for traceability.

## 8. Business Implementation Requirements
- Downstream agents must treat every functional and non-functional requirement in this document as tracing to a Business Rule (Section 6), which in turn traces to a scope_agreement.md or charter.md item.
- Requirements dependent on unresolved open questions (OQ-002 minimum balance, OQ-003 daily cap) must not implement a default numeric limit; implementation must remain configurable/deferred until those questions are answered.
- Exclusion-type business rules (BR-003, BR-008, BR-015, BR-016, BR-017) must not generate downstream functional requirements or FRD entries; they exist to record intentional scope boundaries so traceability checks can distinguish them from coverage gaps.
- All artifacts in this pipeline stage (brd.json, business_rules.json, open_questions.json, BRD.md, BRD.docx) are DRAFT and require human review/approval before being treated as an approved baseline for the next stage.
